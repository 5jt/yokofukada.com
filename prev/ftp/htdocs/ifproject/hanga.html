Crossbay Gallery and Yoko Fukada introduce impressive Japanese HANGA print from the modern to the contemporary, to the Australian art world on 24-26 October 2008 at Sydney Art Fair. The collection is divided into two distinct groups-established artists and artists who are not yet internationally recognized. While the latter group is less familiar abroad, its members have an established reputation within Japan. Fukada has selected their works to be evaluated on the world stage alongside well-known artists Toshinobu Onosato, Yayoi Kusama, Takashi Murakami and others to be enjoyed.

Japanese HANGA, particularly the works of Ukiyoe artists Hokusai and Utamaro, has already been well accepted by the Australian art world. This kind of Ukiyoe woodblock printing flourished during the Edo period (17th-19 th centuries). However, modern Japanese HANGA is less well-known in Australia.

In this exhibition Fukada introduces the new HANGA method, which is an adaptation of the traditional Edo technique and with modern examination demonstrates the full potential of printmaking as art.

The key difference between modern printmaking such as photography and computer graphics (digital images) and woodblock printmaking is that woodblock printing is a multi-layered process. It begins with a two-dimensional paper image and then becomes a three-dimensional wooden object. When it is printed, it is transformed again into a two-dimensional image. This procedure is successively repeated depending on the number of colours in the intended product. Due to this complicated process, HANGA can be considered conceptual.
